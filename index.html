01 Grid-tied PV + storage · North America

Solar captured by day, released at night

A 250 kW / 1.8 MWh lithium storage system — two 125 kW PCS sharing one combiner and control cabinet with six battery packs — working alongside a commercial PV array of eight string inverters on a 480/277 V AC bus. Surplus generation charges the battery through the day and is discharged at night to carry the facility load. PV output above what the battery and the site can absorb is exported to the utility grid.

  • Charging from true surplus only. The EMS compares PV production with site load in real time and diverts only excess generation into the battery, so array yield is never curtailed just to charge.
  • Night discharge against facility load. Stored energy covers evening and overnight demand, moving PV generation into the hours when the site would otherwise buy from the grid.
  • Export once the battery is full. When state of charge reaches its ceiling and daytime load is met, remaining PV output flows to the grid under the site's net-metering or PPA arrangement.
  • Interconnection-ready electrical design. 480/277 V three-phase, 60 Hz, IEEE 1547 grid interface, with metering CTs and the protection, labelling and documentation package the utility's interconnection review asks for.

02 Off-grid microgrid · North America

Solar first, battery through the night, diesel as backstop

A 125 kW / 1.6 MWh battery inside a small off-grid microgrid. Two 50 kW PV inverters and a 50 kVA diesel genset feed a 480 V bus; a static transfer switch and a 100 kVA transformer hand power to the 208/120 V load bus. Surplus PV charges the battery by day and stored energy carries the load at night. When the battery can no longer hold demand, the genset takes over — all scheduled by one cloud-connected EMS.

  • PV is the primary source. Whatever the load does not consume during the day is pushed into the battery, so array output is used rather than curtailed until the battery reaches its ceiling.
  • The battery carries the night. Stored energy serves evening and overnight demand, keeping the genset off for as many hours as the state of charge allows.
  • Diesel starts on a real shortfall. When state of charge reaches its reserve floor and the battery can no longer hold the load, the EMS auto-starts the genset, the static transfer switch closes it onto the bus and the battery recharges inside its efficient loading band — no operator on site.
  • One EMS dispatches all three sources. Source priority, SOC limits, generator start/stop thresholds and charge rates live in a single controller, not in three separate products that have to be coordinated by hand.
  • Remote configuration and diagnostics. The EMS is cloud-connected: setpoints, operating mode and firmware can be changed, and faults read, without sending a technician to site — the deciding factor for sites that are hours from the nearest service base.
